Dinner with a twink and a nerd

2025-05-22

I’ll be late of course. 

They’ll be sitting there, waiting patiently for me to arrive and order for them. I’ll show up and sit across from them. We’ll drink plenty of alcohol and I’ll drink more than them. 

 

I’ll be fine, I always am. I’ll drink confidently and progressively louder. They’ll look at each other and smile, enjoying the flavor of the evening. I’ll also eat most of the food, as twinks eat little, and I don’t know enough about nerds to generalize. 

The restaurant had the décor of a dance studio—not that I was familiar with them, I don’t do that kind of stuff. 

Mirrors ran floor to ceiling, with large gaps for natural light. There were no shadows, for the mirrors threw the light into every corner, and there was no hiding, for the mirrors displayed everyone at different angles. 

 

I’ll sit facing the mirror. That’s my favorite seat; it’s why I eat here so often. Alcohol gets better with a mirror—the reflections are always different.

 

We’ll take the fish because that’s what I want. I’ll look at them and wonder what it’s like to be a twink and a nerd. I’ll chuckle. 

 

“What a weird crowd this is,” I’ll tell myself. 

 

I’ll consider asking what they think people assume our connection to be—given our obvious differences. 

 

This is a cool joint, and I want to be seen as cool here. Sometimes, people make assumptions, and I need it to be clear where I stand. A twink is a twink and a nerd is a nerd and I’m just me. I don’t ask them. Maybe the twink and the nerd haven’t realized this grouping is unusual. 

 

“At least I’m aware of what this looks like,” I’ll resolve to myself, believing that our contrast would work to my favor. “At least I’m across the table.”

 

I’ll go the bathroom towards the end, pissing out the bottle of wine I drank, and look at myself in the mirror. 

 

“You’re so red,” I’ll tell myself. 

 

I’ll grab my hair, pulling it far from my scalp and slap water on my face. There’ll be a pain under my left rib. I’ll shrink and groan and make eye contact with the man in the mirror. There’ll be a moment of silence as the child looking back turns his face and grows scared. There’s a stranger looking at him. 

 

I’ll return to the one-person table facing the mirrored wall and drink my wine quietly. 

 

“I enjoy eating out alone,” I’ll tell my reflection, trying to cheer myself up. “I shall do this again.”
