Once The Sun Rises

2025-01-30

The sun began to shine over the hills, warming his face with the gold morning light. His hair sparkled with the grease of travel. He sat across from me, a small, round table bridging the space between us.

I lit his cigarette as he poured me more coffee. 

The packs almost out, I told him.

We sat there quietly, as I enjoyed the bitter taste of my black coffee, and him, the smooth burn of lit tobacco.

The sun rises later here, he observed.

I agreed, having read somewhere about latitude and light. It warmed my back as it grew higher in the sky.

Enjoy the darkness while you still can, I told him. There’ll be light when you return.

Unfortunately, he added.

We sat in silence, with only the yawns of the early morning birds and hum of electric train rails disturbing our peace.

I thought about the Guernica postcard that sat in my bag, sitting somewhere between the second and third chapters of my Exploring Spain travel guide. The postcard was blank. I bought it for myself.

I read about Picasso during the war, I cut in, breaking our silence.

Oh yea? What about him?

About how painful it was, being stuck in France. About being away from his bleeding land. I think I know how he felt.

Is that why the painting is in gray? Because he was shrouded in the dark?

Perhaps. It’s how I feel anyways. I want to be there, experiencing the light.

You will be. Soon enough.

I know.

Feel this darkness, he tells me. The light will pierce deeper that way.
