complete piq's (#3)

2021-11-21








Prompt: What would you say is your greatest talent or skill? How have you developed and demonstrated that talent over time?


Word Count Minimum/Maximum:


I’m not someone who has a picture-perfect memory, I’m not a music prodigy or anything of the sort. But a skill that I have that I’m grateful for every day is my optimism.


Without intending to sound cliché, I always look on the bright side!


Kidding! But over my 17+ years of existence, living life on the side with the greener grass has been a consistent pillar of my motivation and drive.


Optimism saved me during the pandemic. My parents had split up a few months before, and the horizon was looking bleak. The pandemic was a dismal time for all the things I love to do. I couldn’t see friends, couldn’t play sports, couldn’t live, really. We had to stay home for the entire year. We had to do school three feet from our beds; logging on and logging off for months.


But being optimistic, I saw this year as a glass half full. The year was an opportunity, and I, a taker. I saw the year as one of available time. Time that I could use to explore hobbies and interests. I took extra courses. I picked up reading again. I took time to figure out who I was as a person, and what I wanted to do in my life. The year could have been the Black Plague for me, yet I turned it into the Renaissance.


Optimism for me is less of a skill than a tool. It’s a lens to view life through. It takes appreciating every moment: the good, the bad, and the ugly. Optimism isn’t a tool that I was born with. It’s living, needing to be developed and maintained. It’s a skill that takes overcoming adversity, instead of simply avoiding it. It’s a skill that doesn’t give me anything special in life, just makes life a little more special.
