Manhattan Beach, California

2024-10-31

Dear Blog

There’s a little bit of coffee left in my thermos. It’s a white thermos with Manhattan Beach California written on one side. The paints chipping and the lid is missing. I might go get more coffee, but that would pull me away from this bar stool seat I’ve secured in the law school. It could be a nice study break to walk home and turn the kettle on, but I haven’t done much studying to begin with. I’ve spent most of the morning talking to Colin about my midterm yesterday. It was the US Since 9/11 class I’m in. I was explaining to him what the connection was between September 2001 and November 2024—the moment I’m writing this from, the week before the election.  

I’m afraid, yes. I’m not afraid that he’ll win, I’ve already begun to accept that. I’m afraid of everything changing, of systems changing, and of tradition in this country being uprooted. I guess that’s what it is. I’m afraid of the change. 

But nothing will change for me. I’ll still be a 20-year-old college student with ambition in theory and but lesser in practice. I’ll still be disappointed in how I spend my time, but also happy with the memories I’ve made. There will be little change in my life. 

I’ve finished the coffee in my thermos. I’m comfortable where I am in this seat, looking across at the fraternities on Bancroft. I think I will try and orient myself with the essay I should be writing before I decide it’s happy hour and go grab another coffee.
