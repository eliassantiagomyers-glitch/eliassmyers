Strong Spanish Wind

2025-01-30


The American sat stiffly on the bench. He fidgeted with his coat buttons, struggling to shield himself from this stiff Spanish wind. Finally, he got up.

I watched him approach the lottery booth and ask for the nearest restroom. The booth attendant, (Middle Eastern, I think), didn’t know of one. Maybe he just didn’t know what the American was looking for.

As the American walked past, I called out to him. I told him there was one in the metro station behind us. He thanked me.

“What a different world this is,” he observed. I agreed with him.

“It’s gorgeous.”

“But weird.”

I didn’t respond. I let him disappear behind me. I didn’t know what to tell him. I was already bothered enough.

I had just been in that metro station: Hostafrancs. There was a middle-aged man in there, playing guitar to a jazzy backing. His hair flowed everywhere, and his jacket was dirty.

I knew him. He had played at Plaça Catalunya my first full day here, and I had stopped to listen. Nobody pays attention to the buskers. I caught his name: Mikha. We talked for a few minutes, and he told me he remembered me from the last time. I didn’t have efectivo, but he said I’d paid him enough.

We talked a bit more through our mutually broken Spanish. I think he was Serbian, with a name like Mikha, but he said he’d just moved from Toulouse.

I told him I was going soon.

“It’s a gorgeous city. But very different.”

I walked away. I was already nervous about the trip. The wind was picking up, and I didn’t want to bothered anymore.
