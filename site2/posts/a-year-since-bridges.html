a year since bridges

2024-07-15


I was going through my computer's files this evening when I came across a letter I wrote to myself this time last year. I remember writing this on a bench in the near the hetch hetchy valley on a camping trip with my family. I remember how my mom was there, and my dad was too, and I remember how that was weird. I started this blog around 4 years ago as a letter to myself, something to be an anchor in a life that was rapidly changing. Reading this letter I wrote to myself reminded me of that purpose, and so I thought I'd share it a portion of it here, and cast it into this time capsule. 

I’ve found the need to place my life in a pre-fated timeline. I think that my life will turn out great. I still do, even as I write this. 
I see each event, decision, and change in my life as a step towards the life-completing goal. Every person I meet, every relationship I create, that will be my “___” relationship, friend, experience that I will look back upon to tell myself that my life was complete and that it lived up to every memory goal that I willed it to live up to.
A “memory goal.” That’s an interesting thought. I do have a set of memories that I want. Is that for the purpose of having the memories? What value do the memories even hold, if their purpose is not the contents, yet rather the possession.
I think that I’ve done a convincing job at becoming “someone.” I am interested in how far I missed the mark. If I imitate someone for my entire life, does that still equate to being “someone”? How genuine is “someone” if they commit their entire life to being that “someone”?
As in, I can’t be “fake” or “disingenuine” my entire life. After living a life being that way, would I not have earned the title of truly being that way?
